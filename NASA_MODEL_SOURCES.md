# NASA model and mission-data sources

## Mission-to-model map

| Mission | Spacecraft / surface vehicle used | Launch vehicle used | Provenance and limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apollo 8 | NASA Saturn V GLB; NASA Apollo-Soyuz CSM GLB used as the common Apollo CSM geometry; no LM or rover | Saturn V GLB | Apollo 8 flew a CSM without a lunar lander. The Apollo-Soyuz CSM is a later Block II reference model, not CSM-103's exact exterior markings. |
| Apollo 17 | NASA Saturn V GLB; NASA Apollo Lunar Module GLB; procedural Apollo LRV | Saturn V GLB | The LRV is rebuilt from NASA Apollo 17 press-kit drawings and dimensions; the bundle does not contain an exact NASA LRV GLB. |
| Lunar Orbiter 1 | Mission-specific four-panel Lunar Orbiter reconstruction from NASA descriptions/diagrams | Atlas 6 (Mercury-Atlas) model used only as a related Atlas D-family visual reference; Agena Target Vehicle GLB as an Agena-family reference | Neither GLB is the exact 1966 Atlas-Agena D flight configuration. LO-1 is not replaced by the much later Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter. |
| Surveyor 3 | Mission-specific Surveyor-C reconstruction with three landing legs, TV camera, and sampler arm | Atlas D-family reference plus a procedural Centaur stage | Based on NASA/JPL Surveyor technical drawings and mission data. The bundle has no exact Surveyor 3 GLB; it does not use the Viking lander model. |
| James Webb Space Telescope | NASA/GSFC James Webb Space Telescope (A) GLB | Procedural Ariane 5 ECA stack | The bundle has no NASA Ariane 5 ECA GLB. The launch stack is a data-based reconstruction; it no longer uses the unrelated Shuttle external tank. |

Apollo 11 remains the original reference mission: NASA Saturn V, Apollo LM, and Apollo CSM model assets.

The game’s Mission Flight Hardware option displays each mission’s documented engine family. The High-Thrust and High-Efficiency options are hypothetical gameplay packages; their multipliers, masses, and M-credit costs are balancing values, not proposed or flown NASA engine designs. All mission budgets and subsystem prices in the designer are MissionVerse credits, not historical contract prices.

## Historical science payload baselines

The mission designer now loads a mission-specific baseline package: Apollo 11's PSEP, laser-ranging retroreflector, and crew cameras; Apollo 8's crew cameras only; Apollo 17's LRV, deep-drill / heat-flow package, ALSEP suite, and crew cameras; Lunar Orbiter 1's film imaging system, radiation dosimeter, and micrometeoroid detectors; Surveyor 3's TV camera and soil-mechanics scoop; and Webb's integrated telescope instrument suite. These are educational groupings, not component-level mass models. The mass, power, science points, and M-credit values are gameplay estimates. Alternate packages are visibly marked what-if. Apollo 11 and Apollo 8 have no rover selection or surface rover model.

The liftoff TWR baseline uses documented or approximate full-stack mass and first-stage thrust for Saturn V, Atlas-Agena D, Atlas-Centaur AC-12, and Ariane 5 ECA. Player configuration changes are applied as mass/thrust deltas. Webb's game baseline uses its approximately 301 kg onboard maneuvering propellant, 6,200 kg launch payload mass, and 2,000 W solar-array class; its remaining Δv model stays a simplified educational estimate.

- [NASA Apollo 17 Mission Details](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/apollo/apollo-17-mission-details/) — payload, crew, LRV, and mission results.
- [NASA Lunar Orbiter 1](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/lunar-orbiter-1/) — film imaging, radiation and micrometeoroid measurements, lunar-orbit tracking, and commanded end-of-mission impact.

## Attribution note

NASA 3D Resources describes its models as free to download and use, subject to its usage guidance. NASA is not endorsing this app. The application uses local GLB files and reconstructed geometry; it does not claim that every reconstruction is an original NASA-authored model or a flight-certified, engineering-accurate mesh.

## Official-model availability check

On 2026-11-15, the six NASA GLBs shipped with this project were checked against NASA's official [NASA-3D-Resources repository](https://github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources). The local `saturn-v.glb`, `apollo-lm.glb`, `apollo-csm.glb`, `atlas.glb`, `agena.glb`, and `jwst-a.glb` files are byte-for-byte copies of the repository assets.

The official repository does not contain exact 3D meshes for Lunar Orbiter 1, Surveyor 3, the Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle, Atlas-Centaur, Atlas-Agena D, or Ariane 5 ECA. In this project, those missing pieces are reconstructed from NASA mission documentation or represented with clearly identified related NASA meshes where available. They are not original NASA GLB files. Apollo 8 did not carry a lunar lander or rover; Apollo 17 did carry the Lunar Roving Vehicle.

## Apollo module and uncrewed fairing notes

Apollo 8, 11, and 17 used the same basic Apollo command/service-module design, so their silhouettes should look alike. Their flown spacecraft identities differ: CSM-103 for Apollo 8, CSM-107 *Columbia* for Apollo 11, and CSM-114 *America* for Apollo 17. The bundled Apollo-Soyuz CSM mesh is a general shape reference; it does not reproduce those missions' individual exterior markings.

The tapered shell around Lunar Orbiter 1, Surveyor 3, and JWST in pre-launch views is the payload fairing. It protects an uncrewed spacecraft during ascent and is jettisoned later in the flight; it is not a crew capsule or a separate skirt stage.
